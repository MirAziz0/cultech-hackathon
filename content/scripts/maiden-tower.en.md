# The tower that keeps its secrets

@@ intro
Stop for a moment and look up.

Higher... higher...

This is the Maiden Tower. Almost thirty metres of stone rising from the edge of the Old City. [CHECK: about 29.5 m]

@@ stop:arrival
Every child in Baku knows it. It's on postcards, on banknotes, on souvenirs. [CHECK]

And yet, do you know what's strange?

Nobody knows for sure who built it, or why.

Let's go inside and try to find out together.

@@ stop:buttress
But first, look at its shape.

It's round, but not completely. On one side, a long, thick stone buttress sticks out, like the tower has a spine. Strange, isn't it? Why would anyone build a round tower with a wall sticking out of it?

Hold that question. We'll come back to it.

@@ stop:inscription
High on the tower's wall there's an old inscription in Kufic script. It mentions a name, Masud ibn Davud. [CHECK] Some say this proves the tower was built in the 12th century. Others say the inscription was added later, and the tower itself is much, much older.

So when you stand here, you're standing inside a question that people have been arguing about for a very long time.

@@ stop:entrance
Now, let's enter.

Feel how thick the walls are as you walk through the doorway? Several metres of solid stone. [CHECK: about 5 m at the base] It's cool in here, even on the hottest summer day.

Inside, the tower is divided into floors, one above the other, connected by narrow stairs built inside the thickness of the walls.

@@ stop:well
Now, there's something really interesting inside the tower: a well.

Imagine a narrow shaft going deep down into the rock. Water, inside a tower.

Why would a tower need its own water?

If you were under siege, with enemies outside for days and days, water would mean survival.

Or... maybe this was never a fortress at all?

@@ stop:stair
Let's climb.

Step by step. Watch your head. The windows here are narrow, more like slits in the stone. Only thin beams of light get through.

Now imagine you're a guard, a long time ago, climbing these same stairs in the dark, holding a small oil lamp.

What would you be guarding?

@@ stop:legend
And this is where the stories begin.

Let me tell you the most famous one. But remember: it's a legend, not history.

The legend says that a king once wanted to marry his own daughter. She didn't want to, of course. So she asked him for something impossible: "First, build me a tower so tall that I can see the whole sea from it."

The king built the tower.

The girl climbed to the very top. She looked at the sea... and threw herself into the waves.

And that's why, the legend says, it's called the Maiden Tower.

A sad story, isn't it?

But historians tell us something else. The name may simply mean "untouched", a tower that was never captured by enemies. A maiden fortress. [CHECK]

So which is true? The sad legend, or the proud name?

Maybe both of them, in their own way. People tell legends about places they love.

And here's the biggest mystery.

Some scholars believe the tower was a defensive tower. Others think it was something completely different: perhaps a place connected with ancient fire worship, or even a place for watching the sky and the stars. [CHECK]

@@ stop:roof
And now, the roof.

Come, step out.

Feel that wind? Look out there. The sea!

Today the sea is a little further away, and the boulevard runs along the shore. But long ago, the water came much closer to this tower. [CHECK: how close] Imagine standing here back then: ships, sails, the waves almost at the foot of the walls.

And now look down at that strange buttress again, the "spine" we saw at the beginning.

Some think it made the tower stronger. Some think it pointed in a special direction, toward sunrise on a certain day of the year. [CHECK]

Nobody knows for sure.

And maybe that's what makes the Maiden Tower so special.

It's not a tower that gives you answers. It's a tower that makes you ask questions.

So here's mine for you.

If you could ask the people who built this tower just one question, what would it be?

"Who built you?"

"Why did you make it this shape?"

Or maybe: "Is the legend true?"

Whatever your question is, keep it. Because the Maiden Tower has been keeping its secrets for centuries... and it isn't in any hurry to tell them.
