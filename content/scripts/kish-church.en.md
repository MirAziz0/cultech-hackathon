# Kish Albanian Church: a stone church on the hill, and a 5,000-year-old secret beneath it

@@ intro
Hello! Come on, take it slowly. We're in the mountains here.

Look around: the forested slopes of the Greater Caucasus, cool air, the sound of a river somewhere below.

We're near Shaki, in the village of Kish.

And up ahead, on the hill, stands a stone church: the Kish Albanian Church.

Let me tell you a secret before we start: underneath this church lies a story even older than the church itself.

@@ yard
We're through the gate. Now we're in the stone-paved yard.

Did you see the plaque by the gate? It says "1st–5th century".

But scientists give another date too: the 12th century.

So which one is right?

You know, the sources disagree here. The legend speaks of very ancient times, while the laboratory measured the age of the building that stands here now.

Come on, let's climb the steps and see for ourselves.

@@ court
Now take a close look at the church.

The walls are built of dressed limestone: warm, sand-coloured blocks, row after row.

To the east a semicircular apse, in the middle a tall round drum, and on top of it a cone of orange clay tiles.

See the small triangular gables on the long walls? They give the building the shape of a cross.

Here's a curious detail: the building is taller than it is wide. As if it's stretching up toward the sky.

And look at the bust over there. That's the Norwegian explorer Thor Heyerdahl. He was searching for ancient links between Azerbaijan and Scandinavia. [CHECK: the bust's place and Heyerdahl's link to the project]

@@ crypt
Stop. Look down at your feet.

Under the glass... yes, a human skeleton.

This is an excavation pit. In the early 2000s archaeologists opened the ground here, layer by layer.

Around the church they found graves from the Bronze Age right up to the late Middle Ages.

In the lowest grave, the head points west and the feet point east. That was the Christian custom.

Imagine: for hundreds of years people prayed on this ground, lived here, and were laid to rest right here.

@@ portal
Here's the entrance. On the west side, where the sun sets.

Look at the door: a round arch, and around it a second arch. A double arch.

And above it, a long, narrow window.

Now here's something interesting. Right under this entrance, a grave was found.

Inside were a cross of mother-of-pearl, gilded thread and a piece of gold ornament.

Scientists think a high-ranking cleric was buried here, around the 13th century.

So everyone who came in walked right over him.

@@ nave
We're inside. Cool, quiet, a little dark.

There are no seats here. The church is plain: stone walls, a vaulted ceiling, and light filtering in through narrow windows.

A pair of pilasters divides the hall in two.

Today there are showcases along the walls: pottery from the excavations, crosses, small objects.

Some of them are thousands of years older than this building.

Stop for a moment and look at the pots. Who made them? Who drank water from that jug?

@@ dome
Look up.

Above the four arches are pendentives: curved triangles of stone. They join the square hall to the round drum.

Light streams in through the drum's narrow windows.

For the masons this was no easy task: setting a circle on top of a square.

And there's an interesting idea. Researchers think the drum was rebuilt later, because the proportions of the lower and upper parts don't quite match. [CHECK: the drum's rebuilding]

@@ altar
Now look at the apse. The chancel is higher than the hall.

At first it was only 30 to 40 centimetres higher. Later it was raised to about a metre. [CHECK: sources describe the floor stages differently]

But the biggest secret is right here. Under the altar.

Archaeologists found a cult pit here: pottery fragments, charcoal, burnt bones and two skulls of sheep or goats.

They tested the charcoal in a laboratory. The result: 3360 to 3030 BC.

So about five thousand years ago, people already held this place sacred.

Think about it: a church, before it other beliefs, and before them the people of the Bronze Age... All on the same hill.

**Maybe some places are simply born sacred.**
