## Source conflicts found during research

These came up while collecting facts. Each needs a historian's decision; the app currently hedges them.

- **Shirvanshahs — construction start.** Wikipedia gives a start in 1411 under Ibrahim I, but also says the earliest part dates to the end of the 14th century.
- **Shirvanshahs — 34.15 m² hall area.** Comes from the plan photo on Commons ("Qəbul salonu"), not from a published survey we could read.
- **Shirvanshahs — bay windows, shebeke, alternating masonry courses.** Taken over from the prototype's description; no primary source checked yet.
- **Maiden Tower — wall thickness.** 5 m at the base is consistent; the upper value appears as 3.2–4.2 m, 4 m and 4.5 m in different passages.
- **Maiden Tower — UNESCO dates.** Inscription year given as 2000 (infobox) and 2001 (lead); List in Danger as 2003–2009 and 2004–2009.
- **Maiden Tower — inner stair.** Sources say a staircase "abuts the circular wall"; the Commons photo shows a stair inside a stone passage. The model places it inside the wall thickness as a helix (two storeys per turn) — the exact route is unknown.
- **Maiden Tower — buttress size and the 21 m well's position** (well "discovered on the second floor with an entrance at ground level"). The model puts the well on the ground floor.
- **Erivan Fortress — Sardar's Palace position.** "North-western part" (Erivan Fortress article) vs "north-eastern part" (Sardar's Palace article). The model follows the museum model and the 1827 plan: the palace stands on the gorge (west) side.
- **Erivan Fortress — palace date.** 1798 (Erivan Fortress article) vs "XVI–XVII" (Sardar's Palace infobox).
- **Erivan Fortress — capture date.** The Commons painting title says 1 October 1827 — probably Old Style, which would be 13 October New Style. Confirm which calendar the app should use.
- **Erivan Fortress — Griboyedov performance.** 1827 (Sardar's Palace article) vs a plaque for an 1828 performance (Erivan Fortress article).
- **Erivan Fortress — portraits.** The museum holding them is named as the State Museum of Georgia in one place and the National Gallery of Georgia in another.
- **Erivan Fortress — gates.** Names (Tabriz, Shirvan, Korpu) are sourced; their positions in the model are a hypothesis.
- **Erivan Fortress — Hall of Mirrors dimensions.** Unknown. The model's 9 × 12 m hall with a two-column open front follows Gagarin's lithograph proportions.
- **All monuments — architects.** Usta Rəşid, Usta Fərrux and Usta Kərim are invented representative characters, not historical people, and say so when asked.

## Qız qalası / Maiden Tower (`maiden-tower`)

  - source: [Wikipedia: Maiden Tower (Baku)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maiden_Tower_(Baku))
- [ ] **mt-stair** (evidence-based reconstruction) — A staircase running along the circular wall connects the storeys. Showing it inside the wall in the model is an approximation.
  - source: [Wikipedia: Maiden Tower (Baku)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maiden_Tower_(Baku))
  - source: [Wikimedia Commons: stair between storeys](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Q%C4%B1z_qalas%C4%B1_m%C9%99rt%C9%99b%C9%99l%C9%99raras%C4%B1_pill%C9%99k%C9%99n.JPG)
- [ ] **mt-buttress** (evidence-based reconstruction) — A long solid projection stands on the tower's east side. Some researchers argue it is aligned with the equinox sunrise and had an astronomical purpose; this is a disputed hypothesis.
  - source: [Wikipedia: Maiden Tower (Baku)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maiden_Tower_(Baku))
- [ ] **mt-gutters** (standing / observed) — From the 2nd to the 7th storey, every storey has semicircular ceramic gutters 20–25 cm in diameter.
  - source: [Wikipedia: Maiden Tower (Baku)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maiden_Tower_(Baku))
- [ ] **mt-dating** (evidence-based reconstruction) — The tower's date is debated: some scholars see it as a pre-Islamic fire-temple tower, others date its foundations to the 4th–6th centuries. It is generally agreed that much of today's tower dates from the 12th century.
  - source: [Wikipedia: Maiden Tower (Baku)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maiden_Tower_(Baku))
- [ ] **mt-masonry** (evidence-based reconstruction) — The tower is built of local grey limestone; alternating courses of stone give it a banded look.
  - source: [Wikipedia: Maiden Tower (Baku)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maiden_Tower_(Baku))
- [ ] **mt-unesco** (standing / observed) — The Maiden Tower is on the UNESCO World Heritage List together with the Old City and the Shirvanshahs' Palace; after the 2000 earthquake it was for a while on the List in Danger.
  - source: [UNESCO: Walled City of Baku with the Shirvanshah's Palace and Maiden Tower](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/958/)
  - source: [Wikipedia: Maiden Tower (Baku)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maiden_Tower_(Baku))
- [ ] **mt-foundation** (evidence-based reconstruction) — According to Sara Ashurbeyli's calculations the foundations go 15 m deep; the tower stands on a sloping rock.

## Şirvanşahlar Sarayı / Palace of the Shirvanshahs (`shirvanshahs`)

### Facts marked for verification (8 of 20)

- [ ] **palace-15c** (standing / observed) — The Palace of the Shirvanshahs was built in Baku in the 15th century by the Shirvanshah dynasty. Construction is said to have begun in 1411 under Ibrahim I.
  - source: [Wikipedia: Palace of the Shirvanshahs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palace_of_the_Shirvanshahs)
- [ ] **capital-move** (standing / observed) — Shirvanshah Ibrahim I moved the capital from Shamakhi to Baku after a devastating earthquake.

- [ ] **palace-rooms** (evidence-based reconstruction) — According to sources the palace building originally had 52 rooms: 27 on the first floor and 25 on the second. Today 16 rooms remain on the second floor.
  - source: [Wikipedia: Palace of the Shirvanshahs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palace_of_the_Shirvanshahs)
- [ ] **hall-area** (evidence-based reconstruction) — A published plan gives the central hall's area as 34.15 m².
  - source: [Wikimedia Commons: plan of the reception hall](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:%C5%9Eirvan%C5%9Fahlar_saray_kompleksind%C9%99_ya%C5%9Fay%C4%B1%C5%9F_binas%C4%B1n%C4%B1n_Q%C9%99bul_salonu.jpg)
- [ ] **restoration** (standing / observed) — A major restoration under architect Boris Zasypkin took place in 1932–1934, but the original layout of the upper floor was not restored. A later restoration was completed in 2006.

- [ ] **bay-windows** (evidence-based reconstruction) — According to the descriptions used by the prototype, the palace's south and east facades had bay windows.
